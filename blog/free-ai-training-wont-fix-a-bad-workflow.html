Free AI Training Won't Fix a Bad Workflow

Published July 31, 2026 at 8:40 AM · Stottly Enterprises

OpenAI's new ChatGPT for small business program includes virtual training, in-person academies, and practical support intended to help owners use AI in daily work. Access to credible instruction is valuable. It can reduce intimidation and help teams move beyond random experimentation. It cannot decide which workflow deserves attention, repair unclear ownership, or prevent a business from automating work that should have been removed.

Training often begins with tool capabilities because they are easy to demonstrate. Participants learn to draft, summarize, research, analyze, and automate. They return to work energized and begin applying those capabilities wherever an opportunity appears. Without an operating frame, the result can be dozens of personal shortcuts, inconsistent practices, duplicated subscriptions, and sensitive information moving through tools leadership never reviewed.

Begin with the work, not the feature list. Select one recurring process that consumes time, creates errors, or delays the customer. Map the trigger, steps, decisions, handoffs, systems, and final outcome. Remove unnecessary work first. Clarify who owns the process and what must remain subject to human judgment. Only then should the team decide where AI can improve speed, quality, or access to information.

A useful training program should end with an operating commitment. Define the workflow being tested, the approved data, the responsible owner, the success measure, and the date for review. Keep the pilot small enough to observe. If it saves time, decide where that capacity will go. If it creates rework or weakens quality, stop and redesign it before broader adoption.

The strongest result from AI education is not a room full of employees who can prompt. It is a business that can choose, govern, and improve use cases deliberately. At Stottly Enterprises, training and process design belong together because capability without context spreads activity faster than it creates value.

Small businesses should take advantage of accessible AI education. They should also resist the idea that education is the implementation. A team can learn the tool in an afternoon. Building a reliable way to use it requires a defined problem, a sound workflow, clear guardrails, and leadership willing to measure what changed.
