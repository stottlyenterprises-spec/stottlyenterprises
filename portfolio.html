Flagship Opportunity

Building GM: Our Own Model, Trained In-House

Every other case study on this page is proof we can build for someone else. This one is proof we built for ourselves first. GM is the local-first AI workspace at the core of StottifAI: a model and memory system we designed, trained, and now run on hardware we control, not rented from someone else's cloud.

The goal was never a chatbot with a new coat of paint. It was a system that keeps your models, your memory, and your working context private and portable: yours on the desk where it lives, and yours again from a phone or laptop when you're not at that desk.

  • Local-first architecture, no dependency on third-party inference for core use
  • Persistent memory and project context that travels with you across devices
  • Secure remote access from phone, Mac, or any trusted device
  • A full application shell, not a demo: real navigation, real feature set, built and running
  • The foundation CryptoifAI and future StottifAI products are built on top of

Design philosophy: Build the thing we'd actually want to use, prove it on our own infrastructure, then let it carry the rest of the product line.

Outcome: GM v1 is complete, functional, and live on stottifai.com. It already powers internal workflows, is the base CryptoifAI was built on, and is now ready for client-facing engagements.

Flagship Case Study

Restaurant Operations: From Paper Checklists to a System Managers Actually Use

Context: An international Burger King franchise operator was running shift management the way most of the industry still does: paper checklists, verbal handoffs, and spreadsheets that lived in someone's inbox.

Friction: Shift readiness, food safety, and labor tracking all depended on individual manager habits. Nothing was standardized, nothing was visible past a single shift, and problems surfaced only after they'd already cost time or money.

Intervention: We designed and built a manager-first digital operations hub: daily checklists tied to real standards, real-time issue tracking, shift handoff notes, and labor awareness tools, built around how managers actually move through a shift, not how a corporate system assumes they do.

Adoption: Built for speed and clarity so it would survive contact with a fast-paced kitchen instead of getting abandoned after week one. Multi-lingual support and a visual, low-friction interface removed the usual reasons frontline teams reject new systems.

Result: Delivered to the client and in active use across 12 locations and roughly 50 managers, with a 15% increase in checklist completion rates and an estimated 9,000+ management hours returned annually. The architecture extends cleanly to multi-unit oversight and broader QSR brands. Deployment specifics beyond these figures are withheld under client confidentiality.
